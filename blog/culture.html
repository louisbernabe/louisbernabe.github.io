Welcome to my blog:

Learning Slavic cultures
and Russian language:

My father was a businessman who travelled frequently, to sell grapevines. He had clients all over the world, in countries such as Brazil, Ethiopia, and of course Europe. As a result of demand, many of his clients were from Eastern Europe, in Ukraine, in Russia, as well as in some ex-USSR countries such as Georgia, Romania, Bulgaria.

I missed him during his travels. He always brought back interesting goods from his trips. To facilitate his business in those countries, he decided to learn Russian. I was always intrigued by him learning that language, as it seemed difficult and yet had a fascinating beauty to it.

When I was a teen, around 14 years old, he brought me to the same teacher who helped him, to try it out. I only took a couple of classes, to learn the cyrillic alphabet, and a dozen words or so.

It's when I was in business school in ESADE, Barcelona, that my interest picked up, especially in 2014. I had several Russian and Ukrainian friends who were very interesting, and I saw the opportunity to learn their language. I had a relationship with a Bulgarian girl who was also Russian and she enjoyed seeing me learn cyrillic

I just learned a thousand words at first, which was already challenging. A good rule of thumb when self-learning a language, is that a thousand words is about 95% of spoken language and ten thousand words is about 95% of written language. Therefore, with that fact in mind, I decided to learn to speak a thousand words and to read ten thousand.

Learning grammar was difficult and I would say my grammar is still intermediate. I've also lost track of a lot of words. While I was at ESADE, I learned about 6000 words, by using AnkiApp (now AlgoApp). I would read the Russian orally with my best effort, try to guess the translation, and note my efforts with the app for every word of a 200 word set, until I got an A for the word set (90%+).

By chance, I met a Russian student online who was learning French and I got to language exchange with her at that time, for about a year. She helped me learn to pronounce correctly, which was very difficult to begin with. The hardest letter to pronounce was Щ (shch), though I think I can now pronounce decently even those words with that letter. Some words such as маршрутка (marchroutka) took me a long time.

I spent about 3 years learning Russian at a quick pace, often spending 3-4h/day on exercises of pronunciation and memorisation during summers. As a result, I can still read quite a lot of Russian and I understand some spoken Russian, even without practicing.

The conflict in Ukraine put a stop to my learning, as I was in shock at the brutality of the conflict. More than ever, those people need to be understood. That said, my life has been very tough too and I stopped seeing the returns from learning Russian.

However, I intend to learn more Russian in my life, and in particular, to read Russian books in the original language. There is a great deal of wealth in the Russian culture and in the language as well.

I am therefore hoping for peace in that region of the world, so I can learn more and return there to visit.
